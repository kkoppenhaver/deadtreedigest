The other side of the ledger

Where do the trees actually go?

Short version: Kenya, with a receipt. The longer version is below, and every number in it is checkable.

Every time an issue prints, one tree gets planted. That sentence is easy to put on a website, which is exactly the problem. Lots of companies say something like it, and the trail usually ends at a stock photo of a seedling. Ours ends at a receipt number. This page is the trail.

The mechanism

When your issue comes off the press, our system makes one more call before it goes quiet: a planting request to DigitalHumani, a small reforestation API whose whole job is routing requests like ours to vetted planting organizations. A dollar goes with it. DigitalHumani logs the request, hands back a receipt ID, and passes the planting to TIST in Kenya. We store the receipt next to the issue it paid for. No year-end batch donations, no round-up theater at checkout. One issue, one tree, one receipt.

Who actually plants it

TIST is the International Small Group and Tree Planting Program, which has worked with smallholder farmers in Kenya since 1999. The farmers plant trees on their own land, own them outright, and are paid per tree that is still alive, verified by third parties years after planting. That last clause is why we picked them. A seedling in the ground is a photo op. A tree counted alive years later is a fact.

The math, plainly

A few dozen sheets out. A whole tree back.

An issue runs about 25 sheets of paper. A grown tree yields roughly 8,000. Each issue consumes about a third of one percent of a tree, and we plant a whole one. Call it 300 to 1.

We put it that way, sheets against sheets, because it's arithmetic you can check yourself. You won't find the words "carbon neutral" anywhere on this site. A planted tree is a fact you can count; carbon accounting is somebody else's homework, and we'd rather not turn it in as our own.

So the ratio came down, the honesty went up, and subscriber № 1 got a small forest.
