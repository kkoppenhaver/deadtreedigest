# Dead Tree Digest — Positioning Brief

Decided 2026-07-16 via founder interview. This is the source of truth for all
marketing copy. The writing style guide in claude-code-for-marketers/CLAUDE.md
governs the prose itself: clear, direct, non-AI-ified.

## The positioning statement

For people who are on screens all day and save more than they read, Dead Tree
Digest turns the articles you save into a printed magazine that shows up in
your mail — so the things you meant to read actually get read, somewhere
better than the machine you work on.

## Promise hierarchy

1. **HERO — You'll actually read it.** The product is judged on outcomes:
   things read, not things saved. Saving to DTD is *taking action* on a tab or
   newsletter — close it guilt-free, because it comes back to you on paper.
2. **Second — It arrives as a surprise.** Star of the how-it-works section:
   when you've saved enough to fill an issue, it prints itself and appears in
   your mailbox. No schedule, no notifications, no tracking. You never know
   the day.
3. **Supporting — A different headspace.** Reading happens away from the
   machine you work on: the park, the porch, the train. Different format,
   different mode, better retention.
4. **Seasoning — Longform belongs on paper.** Typeset pages beat scrolling a
   6,000-word blog post. Use sparingly; it's an argument about format, not
   about the reader's life.

NOT the pitch: clutter-relief on its own (it's the *setup*/pain, not the
promise), productivity/inbox-zero framing, anti-screen rebellion.

## Audience

Primary: knowledge workers and builders on screens all day — 20 tabs open,
newsletters starred and forgotten, genuinely curious, whose reading list has
become a guilt list. Secondary: newsletter maximalists (30 Substacks, reads 4
— the inbox is the graveyard). Tertiary nod: the screen-tired.

**Voice-of-customer language (use these, they're the founder's own words):**
tabs I open all the time and never go back to · emails I save in my inbox ·
it just clutters everything · legitimate articles I actually want to read ·
ten more things to read on a computer I'm already on all day · take it to the
park · a different headspace.

## Voice

**Dry wit, sincere core.** The promise is delivered straight; humor is
deadpan, earned by restraint, and lives in footnotes, small print, and the
colophon ("the dead tree is partly fiction; the ten new trees are not").
Never jokey in headlines. No exclamation points. No productivity jargon
(triage, workflow, optimize). No anti-tech sermons.

## The trees

Job: **objection-handler + charm.** One confident line placed exactly where a
reader thinks "isn't printing wasteful?" — e.g. "Every issue plants ten
trees. The math is printed inside." The ledger page is a signature product
detail, not a pitch. BANNED: the "apologize" framing ("we planted ten to
apologize") — retired 2026-07-16 across all materials.

## The name

Two-track (decided 2026-07-16): **Dead Tree Digest stays the working brand**
through first print and site launch prep, always in lockup with an outcome
tagline so the name never has to explain itself. A naming exploration runs in
parallel (outcome-led and artifact/ritual directions; 20+ candidates, domains
checked); final call made with real candidates in hand, before real customers.

## Draft copy directions (to be developed in the site rewrite)

Headline candidates:
- "The articles you save, finally read." — outcome-first, plainest statement of the hero promise
- "Read what you meant to read." — the guilt-list, resolved
- "Your open tabs, printed and delivered." — most concrete; mechanism-forward
- "Saved is not read. This fixes that." — sharpest; dry register

Tagline lockup candidates (for the name):
- Dead Tree Digest — the articles you save, finally read
- Dead Tree Digest — your reading, printed when it's ready

How-it-works skeleton (3 beats):
1. See something worth reading? Save it and close the tab. It's handled.
2. Your saves typeset themselves into a magazine — and when it's full, it prints. You never know the day.
3. It shows up in your mail. Take it somewhere better than your desk.

Trees line (objection slot): "We print on real paper and plant ten trees per
issue for the privilege. The running tally is printed in every copy."
